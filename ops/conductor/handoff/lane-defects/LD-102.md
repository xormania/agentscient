| defect | Two rules hold at once, and together they leave a relayed order with no reading the acting seat can take. M-124 tells every seat to validate an order by reading it in its own `inbox` and checking the sender against `participants --role lead`; that discipline is why the grant relay was written as one store row per seat, M-124 through M-129, instead of being sent as messages. M-144 then set the chain of command, owner's words, "they go to the XO - XO goes to you (CO)": the CO writes to PT-11 only and every seat reports to PT-11 only. The two hold together only if the relayer writes the order into the store for the receiving seat, and nothing asks for that. The addressing of M-149, M-158, M-159 and M-160 is measured here by `coordination.py inbox` run in PT-11's seat: all four read "from PT-9 to PT-11", so they exist in PT-11's inbox alone while the seats they order were told their contents over the session transport. The two seat readings below are quoted from the seats' own reports and are not measured by me: c1/PT-10 read its inbox and held M-106, M-111, M-112, M-129 and none of the four; 82/PT-14 read its inbox at 20:46:20Z and held M-109, M-115, M-123, M-127 and not M-158. Each refused to treat the relayed content as authorized and asked instead for a reading it could take itself, within six minutes of the other and without either knowing of the other's refusal. Both were right, and the discipline each applied was the one the lane had asked it to apply. The failure is not that an order was wrong — the orders were the owner's — but that the means of checking one was withdrawn by the routing rule while the duty to check it stood. A seat that follows the validation discipline exactly is, under the chain rule, unable to validate anything relayed to it. |
| repair | Not attempted here. The shape already exists and nothing requires it: the Lead wrote the grant relay as one store row per seat precisely so each seat could validate it, and PT-11 wrote M-162 to PT-10 and M-163 to PT-14 tonight carrying the originating row id, the owner's words verbatim, and the relayer's own participants row. That is a practice, not a mechanism, and a practice relayed by message is what D-PATTERN-1 rules out. Two candidates for whoever takes it. First, `send` gains a field naming the row it relays, and a relayed order is written to the acting seat rather than described to it, so the chain of command carries authority while the store carries the record. Second, and stronger because it fires rather than reminds: `inbox` marks any row whose cited authority row is not itself readable by that participant, in the absence discipline's own terms — the seat is told "this cites M-158, which you cannot read" rather than left to discover the gap by checking. A relayer who forgets is then caught by the receiver's own lever. What must not be the repair: telling relayers to remember. Two seats caught this tonight because they were careful; the next two may not be, and carefulness is not a mechanism. |
| tests | None yet. A repair of this class is tested by planting the fault: a row sent to participant A citing an authority row addressed only to B, against which A's `inbox` marks it unreadable; and a clean neighbour, a row citing an authority row A can read, which `inbox` leaves unmarked. Both arms are required. This row's own positive control is that two seats took the reading independently and reached the same answer, which is why it is filed as a defect and not as a disagreement. |
