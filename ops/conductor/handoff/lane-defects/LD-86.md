- **status**: open, measured, unowned
- **measured by**: agentscient-db, `gh run view <id> --json jobs` over ten

- **against**: dev d919c982

## What was measured

The conductor suite's per-test cost rose from 115ms on 2026-09-11 to 203ms on
2026-09-16.

| | 2026-09-11 (PR #138, run 34647488620) | 2026-09-16 (run 35108177210) |
|---|---|---|
| tests | 927 | 1478 |
| wall | 106.6s | 299.8s |
| per test | 115ms | 203ms |

The test count rose 59%. At the old per-test cost that is about 170s. The suite
costs 299.8s. The remaining ~130s is the per-test regression and nothing in the
new tests accounts for it.

This is not the whole of the lane job's slowdown, but it is the half that adding
tests does not explain. The other half is volume, and the shard of LD-86's
sibling work (branch lane/session-20260916-ci, wall grant 282) addresses volume
only: sharding divides the 300s among workers, it does not make a test cheaper.
The ~88ms is therefore still paid inside every shard.

## Why it is a row and not a task

The Lead's ruling, 2026-09-16: one deliverable per session. The shard is e0's
deliverable. This row is what the next session takes if the shard alone does not
bring the lane job under the 150s target named in the lock for
lane/session-20260916-ci.

## Where to start

Not measured, named as the first place to look: the conductor tests spawn
`sys.executable` subprocesses heavily. A per-test duration pass over the suite is
the input this row needs and does not have.

It would also settle a question db could not answer inside its timebox. Across
the conductor test files there are 15 `time.sleep` calls; the long arguments (5s,
10s, 120s) are the subprocess targets the tests kill on a timeout, not waits the
suite serves, and actual in-test waits measure 0.02–1.2s — a nominal in-test
sleep budget under 5s against a 300s suite. But five conductor sources under test
do sleep (courier, watch, control, watchdog, monitor), so timeout-driven tests
there wait on a real clock. Which of them, and how much, was not measured. Named
as a gap, not inferred as zero.

## Absence declared

No per-test durations exist for the conductor suite on either date. The 115ms and
203ms are suite wall time divided by test count, which is an average and hides
distribution. Nobody has looked at the distribution; that is "could not look
inside the timebox", not "looked and found it flat".
