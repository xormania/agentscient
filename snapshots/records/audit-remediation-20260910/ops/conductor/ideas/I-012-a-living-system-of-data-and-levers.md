# A living system of data and levers

The lane learns, and Agentscient after it, but the learning lives in
data and in levers, not in a model's memory and not yet in weights.

What exists is the substrate: every act is a record with a measured
outcome. Dispatch records carry cost, wall time and exit; the handwork
ledger carries why a hand was needed; the findings ledger carries what
each reviewer caught and what became of it; the register carries every
ruling with its evidence; the job directories carry, per run, the
rendered prompt, the inputs, the worker's full output and transcript,
the diff and the envelope. Nothing is pruned (the lane conf's retention
line), because these are training data-sets waiting to be derived.

The loop that reads it is calibration, not gradients: measure from the
records, propose a parameter change with evidence, ratify, apply,
re-measure. Bucket sizes from actual minutes and files (scope item 25);
reviewer catch rates from the fixture apps' planted faults; effort per
job from outcome against cost; walls, tick rates, retry limits. Every
such parameter is data in the lane conf, which is why the conf matters.

The spine is the founding rule: the system may change its own
parameters, never its own contracts or gates, without a ratified
ruling, which the workflow genus reserves for one actor; every change
carries evidence and is reversible through the register's supersedes
chain. The hypocrisy concern, declared against practiced, run on the
system itself, is how it notices its own drift.

Learning also lives in levers: a mistake a session made (an edit whose
anchor did not match, a launch under a tool's time cap, a landing by
hand the lever refused) becomes a lever that refuses on the wire, and
the mistake is impossible rather than remembered. "A recurring absence
is a lever demanding to be built" is this rule in the ledger's words.
